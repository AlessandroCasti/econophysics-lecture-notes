## 2. Björk: what now follows the book

The local file `slides_and_others/Arbitrage_Theory_in_Continuous_Time.pdf` is **Tomas Björk, Arbitrage Theory in Continuous Time, fourth edition, 2020**. The old key `Bjork2009` was misleading even though its year field already said 2020. It is now `Bjork2020`, with the fourth-edition ISBN and DOI. [Publisher edition identifier](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198851615.001.0001).

The revised mathematical exposition is a concise adaptation checked against the book, using its notation and explicit source footnotes. It is **not a verbatim transcription of several pages of the book**. Footnotes say which definitions, propositions or theorems the argument follows. This distinction matters: connective explanations written for these lectures should not be passed off as quotations from Björk.

| Material | Book location (printed pagination) | What was done |
|---|---|---|
| Admissible square-integrable Itô integrands; mean, isometry and martingale properties | Definition 4.4, p. 47; Definition 4.7, Proposition 4.8 and Corollary 4.9, p. 50 | Added filtration/measurability and square-integrability conditions; linked the calculus discussion to the later martingale definition |
| Martingale definition | Definition 4.7, p. 50 | Defined conditional expectation with respect to the full information `F_s`, rather than conditioning only on the current value |
| Feynman–Kac | Propositions 5.5–5.6, pp. 74–75 | Used `F(t,x)`, stochastic state `X_s`, coefficients `μ(t,x), σ(t,x)`, a terminal payoff, and integrability of the stochastic integral |
| Self-financing portfolios | Definition 6.10 and the Black–Scholes development | Corrected gains accounting; retained the lecture's option/cash notation outside the added supplement |
| Fundamental theorem and completeness | Theorems 11.10 and 11.17, pp. 159 and 163 | Distinguished NFLVR from informal no-arbitrage language and local from true martingale measures; stated the market/claim restrictions |
| Pricing with a numeraire | Sections 11.8–11.9 | Used `Π_t[X] = B_t E^Q[X/B_T | F_t]` and explained its relation to the lecture's option price |
| Girsanov and Novikov | Theorem 12.3, pp. 177–178; Definition 12.4 and Lemma 12.5, p. 179 | Used the book's likelihood process `L`, kernel `φ`, measures `P,Q`, and Brownian motions `W,W^Q`; included the normalization condition and Novikov's sufficient condition |

The central sign convention is now consistent:

```text
L_t = exp(∫ φ_s dW_s − ½∫ φ_s² ds),     E^P[L_T] = 1,
dQ/dP = L_T,
W_t^Q = W_t − ∫ φ_s ds.

For Black–Scholes: φ = (r − μ)/σ and λ = (μ − r)/σ = −φ.
```

The text explains why existence of the stochastic exponential alone does not guarantee a probability density: the exponential can be only a local martingale. Constant Black–Scholes coefficients satisfy Novikov on a finite horizon.

### Bridges added

1. From Itô integrals to martingales: the mean-zero calculation is placed within a filtration and the required integrability class.
2. From the physical stock model to pricing: Girsanov changes the probability measure, giving risk-neutral drift `r` while retaining the same path space and volatility coefficient.
3. From Björk to the lecture's symbols: the book's claim `X` is distinguished from the lecture's strike `X`; `Π_t[X]` is distinguished from the earlier cash holding `Π(S,t)`; `B` in the supplement denotes the bank account.
4. From the PDE to an expectation: the stochastic state is substituted into the deterministic solution before applying Itô, and the terminal condition is evaluated at the terminal state.
5. From the equity sign convention to bond pricing: the lecture's negative bond diffusion convention is reconciled with Björk's kernel. A constant bond risk premium is stated as an assumption, not inferred from independence of maturity.
6. From a single return to volatility: one absolute increment divided by the square root of its duration does not converge to the instantaneous volatility; integrated squared increments provide the connection to quadratic variation.

### Material that should not be attributed to Björk

The fractional-Brownian/rough-volatility discussion now identifies its own sources and distinguishes antipersistence from long-range dependence. It is not labelled a Björk derivation. The Heston transform and volatility-premium discussion retain attribution to Heston's original paper. Income/Pareto material retains its statistical-physics sources. Adding a Björk footnote to these passages would create another unsupported attribution. [Fractional Brownian motion](https://doi.org/10.1137/1010093), [rough volatility](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2017.1393551), [Heston](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/6.2.327).

## 3. Mathematical corrections beyond the supplement

### Brownian motion and stochastic processes

- Corrected the friction/mobility reciprocal, gravitational drift sign, equilibrium current balance and conservative-potential interpretation. Stokes drag is dissipative and cannot simply be included in the equilibrium potential.
- Restored the integrated Langevin transient. A long-time derivative approximation cannot be integrated back to time zero as though it were exact. Distinguished the differentiable position in the inertial Langevin model from its irregular velocity and from an overdamped Wiener position.
- Repaired the CLT statement: normalized centred sums, nonzero finite variance, convergence in law, and a characteristic-function argument that does not require all moments to exist. Removed the assertion that the summands must themselves be stable and qualified the statement about dependent variables.
- Distinguished an undefined Cauchy mean from an infinite mean. Excluded the Gaussian endpoint from the stable power-law tail formula.
- Corrected the local Gaussian approximation to the random walk. Only sites of the right parity are reachable, so their spacing is two: lattice probability is approximately twice the continuous density. The physical density limit divides by `2 Δx`, with explicit joint drift/diffusion scaling.
- Distinguished consistency of finite-dimensional distributions from existence of a continuous version. Kolmogorov's 1931 analytic-methods article is cited in the forward-equation context, not as the source of the extension theorem.
- Corrected the continuity and nondifferentiability probability calculations, including factors in Gaussian tails. A fixed-time calculation is not a proof of nowhere differentiability; continuity in probability is not itself almost-sure path continuity.
- Corrected the Brownian correlation limit and qualified the formal path-integral expression for state-dependent diffusion. Retained the lecture's formal development rather than presenting it as a fully general rigorous theorem.
- Clarified that the Kramers–Moyal Taylor derivatives hold the increment fixed. With `Δ = x − x′`, the Taylor factor is `(-Δ)^m`.

### Stochastic calculus and derivatives

- Distinguished quadratic variation from a single squared increment. The variance of the squared increment is of order `Δt²`, while its standard deviation is of order `Δt`.
- Made white noise's normalization and distributional meaning explicit. Replaced the inappropriate “Doob transform” label for a linear integrating factor.
- Corrected assertions about stationarity of an OU process started from a fixed value: its distribution converges to the invariant law, but it is not stationary from the start.
- Corrected reversed put-exercise cash flows, unrestricted divisible holdings, and the unnecessary small-rate condition for continuous compounding.
- Distinguished the stock's historical drift from a no-arbitrage pricing condition. Removed claims that GBM is forced by market efficiency or is the only analytically tractable model.
- Corrected the standard calendar-time Theta sign and its relation to time to maturity; Theta is not universally negative.
- Explained that `N(d₂)` is the exercise probability under the bank-account measure, while `N(d₁)` is associated with the stock numeraire and is also the call delta.
- Specified the assumptions for European put–call parity and the nonnegative-rate condition in the no-early-exercise result for a non-dividend-paying American call.
- Added the CRR no-arbitrage interval, distinguished recombination from the reciprocal-jump convention, and made variance matching asymptotic. Removed the impractical fixed recommendation to build an enormous quadratic-size tree.
- Repaired barrier-tree terminal indexing, initialization and equality-at-the-barrier conventions; distinguished discrete monitoring from continuous crossing. Corrected the American continuation value, which is not generally a European option value.

### Rates, advanced models, and income

- Corrected the bond hedge's sign and distinguished holdings from bond values. Specified the conditional risk-neutral expectation with unit terminal bond payoff.
- Corrected short-time OU mean expansion, conditional distribution notation and historical versus risk-neutral mean levels.
- Repaired the special squared-OU construction of CIR, including the Brownian sign transformation and missing factor two. It does not derive the entire CIR parameter family. Separated nonnegativity, strict positivity under the Feller condition, transition distribution and invariant distribution.
- Corrected the intermediate integral for the affine bond coefficient; the final closed form was already correct. Replaced exact matching of an overdetermined term structure with least-squares calibration language.
- Corrected Gaussian convolution normalization, Fourier dummy variables, stable-law location/scale language and the Student mixture: the variance mixing variable is inverse Gamma, not chi-square.
- Corrected centred, normalized return and absolute-return autocorrelations. An uncentred absolute-return moment does not decay to zero. Removed assertions that a fixed guide curve had been fitted to the displayed data.
- Corrected the fBM discussion: `H > 1/2` gives long-range dependence of increments; `H < 1/2` gives antipersistence with summable increment correlations. Small-H roughness is not the same phenomenon as positive long memory. Markovianity and market efficiency do not imply the claims made in the old expansion.
- Corrected Heston self-financing accounting, the nonzero-vega hedge condition, physical versus pricing drift, and the meaning of the volatility risk premium. Restricted the quoted affine transform to a compatible affine premium and supplied an actual Fourier inversion expression in place of an unspecified integrand.
- Distinguished income from wealth. Replaced global second moments missing drift/boundary terms with local conditional increment moments. Corrected the probability-current sign and replaced an absorbing-style condition inconsistent with exponential equilibrium by zero reflecting current. Removed an unjustified money-conservation assumption for the income model.
